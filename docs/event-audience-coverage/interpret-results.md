# How to interpret and act on an audit

## Coverage scores

| Value | Meaning | Editorial response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `0` | Relevant audience has no meaningful route or value communicated | Consider a targeted addition |
| `1` | Relevance only implicit | Explain why it matters to that audience |
| `2` | Partially covered | Improve outcome, evidence, or next step |
| `3` | Clear relevance and action | Maintain and refine |
| `4` | Strong coverage with convincing evidence and path forward | Reuse this pattern thoughtfully |
| `N/A` | Not an intended/reasonable audience for this page | Do not penalise |
| `?` | Page or evidence was not verifiable | Investigate; **not** a zero |

Use the skill's [scoring rubric](../../skills/event-audience-coverage/references/scoring-rubric.md) for the full rules.

## Turn recommendations into work

1. Prioritise missing primary audiences on major navigation or conversion pages.
2. For each improvement, capture the URL, persona, observed evidence, proposed edit, owner, priority, and acceptance criterion.
3. Maintain page-specific relevance: do not turn technical sessions into generic marketing copy.
4. Review proposed factual claims, speaker details, programme changes, and ticket information before publication.
5. Save the report and matrix with an audit date; repeat with comparable scope to evaluate change.

## Don't overinterpret

The audit measures **content signals**, not who actually attended or converted. A strong score is not proof of audience reach. Combine with analytics or survey data only when those data are supplied and appropriately sourced.
