# Inclusive event content guidance

- Say what visitors will **learn, build, decide, discover, or connect with**; avoid feature-only jargon.
- Explain Drupal briefly where newcomer comprehension matters; do not dilute deep technical articles.
- Avoid positioning attendance as conditional on being a Drupal expert or contributor.
- Speak to buyer versus seller needs separately: evaluating technology is not the same as promoting services.
- Connect AI to real challenges, concrete deployments, trade-offs, and responsible practice; avoid inflated claims.
- Demonstrate product and SaaS relevance through integrations, ecosystems, opportunities and case studies where supported.
- Highlight design, editorial, accessibility, nonprofit, public-sector and academic viewpoints where genuine.
- Identify explicit audience fit, prerequisites, outcomes and suitable next steps for sessions.
- Avoid unsupported claims about expected attendees, ROI, speaker availability, partnerships or ticket benefits.
- Recommend small targeted edits when sufficient; do not propose a new page unless existing navigation cannot reasonably carry the gap.
- Distinguish content audit from technical accessibility or SEO audits: call out obvious textual issues but do not claim WCAG compliance testing.
- Respect editorial voice: thoughtful, direct, community-minded, technically credible, not overly promotional.
